book

Deep and Dark and Dangerous

by Mary Downing Hahn

Summary: Thirteen-year-old Ali is looking forward to a quiet summer with her aunt and young cousin at the family's old lake house in Maine. But an unsettling local girl keeps inserting herself into their vacation, and Ali starts to sense there's something she's not being told about her own family's past at that lake.

About the author: Mary Downing Hahn is an American author and former children's librarian best known for ghost stories written for young readers, including Wait Till Helen Comes and Took. Her books have won more than fifty state children's choice awards over her career.

Deep and Dark and Dangerous is a well-written novel, but it falls victim to predictability and an unsatisfying ending. The moment Sissy shows up, it's obvious she must be the girl from the torn photo — the mystery never really gets a chance to breathe. When Ali finally puts the pieces together, it feels rushed, like it's all pulled from the previous chapter at once. The detail with the flower petals thrown into the lake "proving" Sissy is Teresa felt especially random. The story just isn't mysterious enough, and it doesn't ask the reader to think very hard to get there.

The ending doesn't land, either. Dulcie tells everyone the truth about what happened to Sissy/Teresa, but it doesn't feel like anything is really accomplished by that point. I think what I wanted was a scarier story — something where Teresa's ghost tries to pull one of the girls down to the bottom of the lake so they can be ghosts together forever. Instead, Teresa just wanted justice. She gets it in the end, but I wasn't satisfied, because I wanted it to be scarier.

That said, I think Mary Downing Hahn nailed exactly what she set out to do: tell a ghost story. Sissy is constantly described as having almost ghost-like blonde hair, which I really liked, and there's a recurring focus on how thin the characters are that adds to that eerie, bones-under-the-skin feeling. It's a genuinely good book — I'd just recommend it for a younger audience than myself.

3/5 ★
